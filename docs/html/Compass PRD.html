Compass: what it must do

A personal planning system for one person. It makes sure that what you save to learn comes back until you decide on it, and that each day’s work traces to a goal you chose.

PRD draft 1.0One userMac · Android · Telegram70 requirements

Ask

Four product calls for you

  1. Are the targets in section 03 the right bar?
  2. Should “learned” require a one-line note? I have said yes.
  3. Learning slots: Mon, Wed, Fri at 16:00 and Sat at 10:00. Right for you?
  4. Do life goals sit beside work goals? I have assumed yes, as in the mockup.

Carry

Already decided

  • For you alone, on your Mac, at no cost.
  • The phone side is a Telegram bot.
  • Meetings come from Google Calendar.
  • AI features stay off for now.
  • The approved mockup is the design reference.

Risk

What would make this fail

Overhead. The product asks for about an hour and a half of planning a week. If that feels like work about work, it gets dropped. So the first release asks for nothing: you share links, and it brings them back.

01

The problem

Two problems, in your words, and they feed each other.

ProblemWhat happens todayWhat it costs
Saved, then forgottenYou meet something worth learning almost every day and mean to come back to it. There is no place it goes and nothing brings it back, so by your account almost none of it gets looked at again.The learning does not happen, and the list of things you meant to learn becomes a source of guilt.
No plan, so no directionDays, weeks, months and the year are not planned. Without a plan there is nothing to set goals against and nothing to measure a day by.Work fills the time that is available. Effort is high, and it is hard to say what it added up to.

They are one problem seen from two sides. Learning never gets time because time is never allocated on purpose, and time is never allocated on purpose because there are no goals to allocate it to.

Why a custom tool

Task apps store tasks and read-later apps store links. Both are good at holding things and neither makes you decide. The gap is a system that forces a decision on every saved item and connects each day’s work to a goal. That behaviour is the product. Everything else is plumbing.

02

User and context

There is one user. The product is built around how you already work, and it does not have to suit anyone else.

FactWhat it means for the product
Founder and engineer, mostly working aloneNobody else assigns priorities. The product has to supply the structure a manager or a team would.
Mac at the desk, Android phone away from itPlanning and review happen on the Mac. The phone is for capture and short answers.
Telegram is already on the phoneThe phone side is a Telegram chat. There is nothing new to install or open.
Meetings live in Google CalendarCompass plans around meetings and does not try to own them.
Trading hours shape the dayThe day view marks the NSE open and close, since deep work and market attention compete.
Interesting links arrive all day, from many appsCapture has to work from any app in two taps and must never ask a question first.

The jobs it is hired for

  1. When I come across something worth learning, I want to save it in seconds, so that I can get back to what I was doing without losing it.
  2. When I have saved something, I want it to come back to me until I decide, so that forgetting is never the outcome.
  3. When I start a day, I want to know the three things that matter and why, so that the day adds up to something.
  4. When a week ends, I want to see where the time went against what I intended, so that next week is better aimed.

Guardrails

Not goals

Working for other people. Replacing the calendar. Storing notes or documents. Reading saved content inside the app. Tracking habits in general. Summaries or suggestions from AI, which stay off for now.

04

Product principles

When a requirement is unclear or two of them pull apart, these decide.

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Capture is free, decisions are forcedSaving asks for nothing. After that the system keeps asking until you book it, link it or let it go.
The system does the rememberingYou never have to recall that something exists. If it matters today, it is on your desk today.
Every task has a whyWherever a task appears, the goal it serves is one glance away. Untied work is allowed and is counted.
Letting go is a successDropping an item on purpose is a decision made. Only silent expiry counts against you.
Few things at onceThree priorities a day, three outcomes a week, three themes a year, three nudges a day.
Calm by defaultOne quiet colour, attention colour only where something needs you, detail on click.
Plans are cheap to changeMoving a task is one drag. A plan that is hard to change is one that gets ignored.

05

Scope and releases

Five releases. Each one is usable by itself, and the first asks nothing of you except to share links. This table says what you can do after each. The requirements in section 06 carry the release they belong to.

ReleaseAfter it, you canProblem it addresses
P0 Core loopShare a link from your phone or Mac, see it in a queue, and have it come back on days 3, 7 and 14 until you decide. Get a morning brief in Telegram.Forgetting
P1 DirectionSet themes, goals and weekly outcomes. Pick a top three each day and see what each task leads to. Plan the week on a board. Close the day.Direction
P2 TimeSee meetings and blocks in one day view. Have four learning blocks a week appear on your calendar already filled. Get reminders.Both
P3 ReflectionOpen Sunday’s review with the numbers filled in. See pace on quarter goals and your streaks.Direction
P4 PolishSave a browser tab in one click. Check system status. Export everything.Comfort

Two changes from the build order in the architecture study, both deliberate. The morning brief moves to P0, because the bot already exists there and the brief is what makes resurfacing visible on the phone. The daily backup moves to P1, because from that release the file holds goals you would not want to retype.

06

Requirements

Ten areas. Each has the need it serves, numbered requirements with a priority and a release, and the tests that say it is done. Must means the release is not finished without it.

A. Capture

When I see something worth learning, I want to save it without stopping what I am doing.

IDRequirementPriorityRelease
CAP-1On the Mac, pressing C on any screen opens quick capture. A title or a link alone is enough to save.MustP0
CAP-2On the phone, sharing a link or text to the Compass bot saves it. The bot replies “Saved” once the Mac has stored it.MustP0
CAP-3A link becomes a learning item. Plain text gets a one-tap choice: learn later, task or note.MustP0
CAP-4Saving a link that is already open in the queue does not create a second card. The existing item moves to the top of the revisit list and the reply says when it was first saved.MustP0
CAP-5Captures sent while the Mac is closed arrive at the next wake, in the order sent, if that is within 24 hours.MustP0
CAP-6Each learning item gets a title from the page, a source type and a time estimate. The estimate defaults to 20 minutes and can be edited.ShouldP0
CAP-7A goal and a one-line reason can be added at capture. Neither is required.CouldP1
CAP-8A browser extension saves the current tab in one click.ShouldP4

Accepted when

  • With the Mac closed, a link shared from the Android share sheet is in the inbox within a minute of opening the lid, and the chat shows “Saved”.
  • Sharing the same link twice leaves one card in the queue.
  • From any screen on the Mac, C, paste, Enter saves an item in under five seconds.

IDRequirementPriorityRelease

LQ-2Opening a card offers only the moves that are valid for its state: book, link a goal, let go; start, unbook; mark learned.MustP0
LQ-3Dragging a card to another column makes the same move as the matching button.MustP0

  • A let-go item never appears again in any list or nudge.

C. Autopilot

When I have saved something and done nothing, I want the system to bring it back, and to find time for it without my asking.

IDRequirementPriorityRelease
AP-1An inbox item with no decision returns to “Time to revisit” on days 3, 7 and 14 after capture.MustP0
AP-2On day 21 the item gets a last ask. With no answer in two days it is archived as expired.MustP0
AP-3The revisit list shows at most three items a day: last asks first, then the oldest.MustP0
AP-4If the Mac was closed when a run was due, the next run surfaces exactly what the missed one would have.MustP0
AP-5Open tasks at shutdown each need one decision: move to tomorrow, reschedule or drop. Nothing carries over silently.MustP1
AP-6Four learning slots a week are protected on the calendar. Days, times and length are settings.MustP2
AP-7Each empty slot is filled with the best item that fits: one already started, then goal-linked, then older. One tap swaps it.MustP2
AP-8A learning block that passes without being started returns its item to the top of the revisit list and counts as not kept.ShouldP2

Accepted when

  • A link saved on a Monday and ignored appears in Thursday’s revisit list without any action from you.
  • After five days with the Mac closed, opening it shows the same revisit list an always-on system would show that day.
  • On Sunday evening, next week’s four learning blocks are on the calendar with an item in each, if the queue has enough that fit.

Rituals and time budget

The product asks for your time at fixed moments. Together they must stay under two hours a week, or the system becomes the work.

RitualWhenTimeWhat it produces
CaptureWhenever something catches your eye5 secondsAn item in the inbox
Morning briefFirst wake of the day1 minuteDecisions on up to three saved items
Learning blockFour times a week30 to 45 minutesOne item learned, with a note
Evening shutdown19:3010 minutesA closed day and tomorrow’s top three
Weekly reviewSunday20 minutesA closed week and next week’s outcomes
Month checkLast day of the month15 minutesNext month’s milestones
Quarter planningLast week of the quarter60 minutesUp to five goals with key results
Year planningDecember90 minutesThree themes and first-quarter goals

In a normal week that is about 70 minutes of shutdowns, 20 of review and a few of briefs, which comes to roughly an hour and a half. Learning blocks are not counted as overhead. They are the point.

09

Edge cases and empty states

SituationWhat the product does
First day, nothing savedToday shows one prompt: share your first link to the bot. No empty charts.
No goals set yet (P0)Tasks and items work without goals. Goal fields stay hidden until P1 setup is done.
Mac closed for a weekendAt wake, overdue runs happen in order and one combined brief is sent.
Mac closed for more than a dayLinks shared in the gap may be lost. Any message in the chat without “Saved” needs sharing again.
The queue has nothing that fits a slotThe block stays on the calendar, marked open. The reminder suggests the shortest inbox item.
A meeting lands on a learning slotThe block moves to the next free 45 minutes that day. If there is none, it counts as not kept and the item returns to the list.
More than three things want to nudgeThree are sent, by priority. The rest wait for the next brief.
A fourth task is ranked into the top threeYou are asked which one it replaces.
A goal is deleted with tasks under itIts tasks become untied and its child goals ask for a new parent.
You are travelling in another time zoneDays stay on Asia/Kolkata until you change the setting.
A page title cannot be fetchedThe item keeps the raw link as its title and can be renamed.

11

Risks and open questions

RiskWhy it mattersResponse
High Planning feels like overheadThe rituals get skipped, then the appA hard two-hour weekly budget. P0 asks for nothing and only gives things back.
High The inbox still piles upResurfacing becomes noise you swipe awayThree items a day at most, a last ask, automatic expiry, and queue flow in the weekly review.
Medium Nudges get mutedThe phone side of the loop is lostThree a day, each answerable in one tap, one combined brief after a long sleep.
Medium Goals are set once and go staleThe trace points at goals that no longer matterMonth, quarter and year planning prompts, and pace shown on every quarter goal.
Medium Scope grows into a notes appThe core loop stallsNotes stay plain text. The not-goals list in section 03 is the guard.

Dependencies

Telegram for the phone side, Google Calendar for meetings and blocks, Google Drive for desktop for backups. All three are free services outside your control. The queue, goals and reviews keep working on the Mac without any of them.

Later, not now

AI titles, summaries and estimates. The full app on the phone. Spaced repetition for what you have learned. Month and quarter reviews in the same form as the weekly one. Each can be added without changing anything above.
